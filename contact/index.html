CONTACT PRINCIPLE

Make it clear from the beginning what you are contacting us about

Mentioning the inquiry category at the beginning of a message helps identify the issue quickly. For example— Correction, Copyright, Content Permission, Source Clarification, or Documentary Suggestion.

Instead of sending a vague message such as “I wanted to say something” or sharing a link without context, briefly explain the issue.

Provide only the information that is necessary— but do not send passwords, OTPs, banking credentials, or unnecessary sensitive information.

FACTUAL CORRECTION

The more specific a correction request is, the more effective the review can be

If you believe a documentary or article contains a material factual error, do not stop at saying only “this is wrong.” Include the disputed statement, publication link, and supporting evidence.

For video content, including a timestamp makes the relevant section easier to locate.

If reliable primary, institutional, academic, or other credible evidence is available, providing a direct link is helpful.

COPYRIGHT CONCERN

If you have a rights concern, clearly identify the material

If you are a photographer, videographer, archive, publisher, institution, or other rights holder and believe your material has been used in a SalimGPT publication with incorrect permission, attribution, or usage context, you may raise a concern.

To identify the material, it is important to include the publication link, screenshot, timestamp, original source, and the basis of your rights.

Rather than forwarding only a third-party claim, provide supporting information about ownership or authorization whenever possible.

CONTENT PERMISSION

If you request reuse permission, explain how the material will be used

If you want to reuse a SalimGPT documentary clip, written content, image, thumbnail, graphic, or other substantial content, specify which material, how much of it, on which platform, for what purpose, and whether the use is commercial.

It is also relevant to state whether the content will be modified, translated, dubbed, republished, or redistributed.

DOCUMENTARY SUGGESTION

Do not send only a topic— explain why it should become a documentary

If you want to suggest a documentary topic, include not only the topic name but also why it matters, its public-interest value, which questions could be explored, and where credible sources may be found. This makes the suggestion more useful.

Accepting a suggestion does not guarantee that the topic will enter production. Research feasibility, evidence availability, originality, public interest, and production suitability may all be considered.
