CORE PRINCIPLE

Using someone else's work does not make it SalimGPT's property

If an image, footage, archive, illustration, music, recording, or other third-party material is edited, cropped, color-graded, animated, or transformed with AI for a SalimGPT documentary, that alone does not automatically transfer the original rights to SalimGPT.

The rights of the original copyright holder, license terms, permission conditions, and applicable law may continue to apply. Third-party material and SalimGPT-created production assets are therefore not treated as the same type of ownership.

SalimGPT does not treat unauthorized use as lawful merely because material has been edited or transformed.

IMAGE-TO-VIDEO

When footage is created from an image, the rights to the source image also matter

SalimGPT may create AI-assisted motion footage from a still image. Before an image-to-video process, it is important to have an appropriate usage right for the source image.

If the source image is SalimGPT's own generated asset, it may be used in production according to the applicable AI tool terms.

If the source image belongs to another person, photographer, archive, agency, or institution, the necessary permission, license, or lawful-use basis must be considered separately.

THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL

Using another party's image or footage requires a separate rights process

A documentary topic may require directly relevant archival material, photographs, footage, illustrations, or other media.

In such cases, SalimGPT may use material according to permissions or license conditions provided by the rights holder, creator, archive, agency, institution, or authorized source.

Some permissions may be free, some may require attribution, some material may require a commercial license, and some usage rights may be obtained through payment.

SalimGPT's policy is to use material according to the conditions under which permission was obtained.

ATTRIBUTION & CREDIT

Where credit is required, follow the source conditions

For some licensed or permission-based material, crediting the creator, photographer, archive, institution, or rights holder may be a condition of the license.

Attribution may appear in on-screen text, a description, a website reference, end credits, or another appropriate location. The exact format may vary depending on the source conditions and presentation.

Providing credit does not mean ownership of copyright is transferred. It acknowledges the rights and contribution of the original creator or source.

RIGHTS MANAGEMENT

A single documentary may contain several different rights

A documentary script may be original to SalimGPT, narration may be synthetic, some footage may be SalimGPT-generated assets, some images may come from licensed archives, and other material may be in the public domain.

For that reason, it is not always accurate to place the entire production under one ownership category.

SalimGPT may hold applicable rights in the composition of the final documentary, while underlying rights in third-party assets used within it may remain with their respective owners.

PUBLIC DOMAIN

“Old” does not automatically mean public domain

A historical image or piece of footage does not automatically become copyright-free simply because it is old.

Legal status may differ according to copyright duration, publication history, jurisdiction, and source status.

When public-domain material is used, SalimGPT's approach is to verify available rights information as far as reasonably possible.

ARCHIVAL MATERIAL

The archive source and copyright owner may not be the same entity

If a museum, archive, university collection, or website hosts a historical image, that alone does not mean the institution owns every underlying copyright.

Access rules, reproduction policies, metadata, creator information, and rights statements may need to be reviewed separately.

Similarly, online access does not automatically grant permission for commercial reuse in a documentary.

LEGAL EXCEPTIONS

Fair use or similar exceptions are not universal permission

Different jurisdictions may provide copyright exceptions for quotation, criticism, review, reporting, education, or other limited uses.

However, “fair use” or an equivalent concept does not operate identically in every country, and no use becomes automatically lawful merely by applying that label.

SalimGPT's preferred approach, where practicable, is to rely on clear permission, licensing, public-domain status, or another defensible lawful-use basis.
