FACT-CHECKING & SOURCES

Finding information and verifying information are not the same thing

In SalimGPT documentary research, information is not automatically treated as a final fact simply because it appears on a website, in a search result, or in an AI response. Important claims, historical events, dates, statistics, scientific explanations, and controversial information are compared whenever possible with multiple sources, original context, and chronology.

CORE PRINCIPLE

Information from one source is not always the complete truth

Different sources may describe the same event with different wording, dates, incomplete context, or different interpretations. In SalimGPT's research process, source quality and context matter as much as the number of sources.

Information is not accepted simply because it is convenient for the documentary narrative. Instead, the questions are: What evidence supports the claim? Where is the original source? Do independent sources support it? And does newer or stronger evidence change the conclusion?

The goal is not to find “information that makes the story better,” but to build the story around the evidence that exists.

SOURCE QUALITY

Not every source provides the same kind of evidence

The usefulness of a source depends on the documentary topic and the specific claim. Historical questions, scientific data, legal information, contemporary events, and personal statements may each require different kinds of sources.

For example, an original agency report may be most useful for an official statistic; an archive or original publication may be most useful for a historical quotation; and peer-reviewed research or a recognized institution may be more appropriate for scientific explanation.

SalimGPT therefore does not blindly follow a rigid source ranking. The aim is to identify the most relevant and authoritative evidence for the specific claim.

SEARCH ENGINE ROLE

Google is not a source— it is a way to find sources

Google Search or another search engine may help locate a topic, person, institution, archive, or document during the early stages of research.

But a search-result snippet, AI summary, or headline is not sufficient by itself as final evidence. When necessary, the original page, report, document, or source is opened to examine the information in context.

Similarly, appearing near the top of search results does not automatically guarantee that a website is reliable.

CROSS-CHECKING

Important claims are checked against multiple independent sources whenever possible

The purpose of cross-checking is to determine whether the same incorrect information is simply being repeated across multiple websites. Many websites may copy information from the same original source rather than conduct independent reporting.

Finding the same sentence on three websites does not necessarily mean there are three independent confirmations. When possible, SalimGPT checks whether the sources rely on separate evidence or on the same original source.

Source independence is especially important for major historical claims, controversial allegations, casualty figures, scientific conclusions, or sensitive information.

CHRONOLOGY CHECK

Accurate information can still be misleading when placed in the wrong timeline

In historical documentaries, it is not enough for individual events to be accurate. Which event happened first, what happened later, and whether one event actually caused another also matter.

When working with dates, years, reigns, conflicts, inventions, policy changes, or social transformations, available timelines are compared whenever possible.

Causal statements such as “this event caused that event” require separate consideration of chronological sequence and supporting evidence.

NUMBERS & STATISTICS

A number can easily mislead without context

When using percentages, death tolls, population figures, economic figures, prevalence estimates, scientific measurements, or survey results, it is important to understand not only the number itself but also its definition, year, sample, source, and scope.

For example, whether a percentage represents an entire country or a small sample, whether a statistic is annual or cumulative, and whether a figure is estimated or confirmed can completely change the meaning of the final script.

CONFLICTING SOURCES

When two sources disagree, one is not simply chosen at random

Conflicting information is not unusual during research. Dates, casualty figures, origins, or interpretations of the same event may differ across sources.

In such cases, publication date, methodology, proximity to the event, cited evidence, institutional credibility, and newer corrections may all be considered.

If a conflict cannot be fully resolved, acknowledging uncertainty or disagreement in the final script may be more honest.

QUOTES & STATEMENTS

The words in a quotation matter— and so does the surrounding context

When using a statement from a public figure, researcher, institution, or historical source, SalimGPT aims to understand who said it, when it was said, and in what context.

A few words should not be extracted from a longer statement in a way that creates a meaning inconsistent with the original statement.

Where exact quotation is not necessary, information may be paraphrased while preserving its meaning and source context.

AI ≠ SOURCE
AI-ASSISTED RESEARCH

AI can assist research, but an AI response is not itself a final source

In SalimGPT's research workflow, AI may assist with topic exploration, terminology, search direction, summarization, translation, or research organization.

But AI-generated answers may contain factual errors, outdated information, missing context, or fabricated references. Important claims are therefore not taken into the final script solely on the basis of an AI response.

If AI points toward a report, paper, or historical source, the original source may be located and independently checked when necessary.

FACT vs INTERPRETATION

Information and interpretation should not be presented as the same thing

FACT

Evidence-supported information

Dates, events, records, measurements, documented statements, or other verifiable information.

INTERPRETATION

Meaning, analysis & context

Historical, social, or editorial explanation based on evidence.

Interpretation may be necessary for documentary storytelling. But ensuring that interpretation does not replace verified facts is an important part of editorial review.

SOURCE RECORD

Important references are tracked whenever possible

During documentary research, important source links, report titles, authors, publication dates, archives, institutions, or other relevant references may be retained when necessary.

The purpose is to make it easier to revisit the research trail during script review, future corrections, source attribution, or factual questions.

FIX WHEN NEEDED
CORRECTION APPROACH

Correcting published information when it is proven wrong is not a weakness

SalimGPT does not claim to be free from error. No matter how careful the research, a source may be wrong, new evidence may emerge, or a factual mistake may remain in production.

If a significant factual error is identified through credible evidence, SalimGPT may attempt to correct the article, description, future edition, or relevant publication as appropriate.

Similarly, when new research changes an earlier conclusion, considering updated evidence is part of editorial responsibility.

LIMITATIONS

Fact-checking does not mean claiming that errors are impossible

Historical records may be incomplete, scientific evidence may change, official statistics may be revised, and reliable sources may disagree about the same event.

SalimGPT therefore does not aim to claim absolute certainty. The goal is to provide the most accurate, context-aware, and transparent explanation possible based on the evidence available at the time of publication.

Where uncertainty matters, wording such as “estimated,” “reported,” “according to,” or equivalent contextual language may be used instead of presenting uncertainty as certainty.

HUMAN EDITORIAL CONTROL

Final source judgment is a human decision

Search engines, AI, databases, or software can help locate sources. But the final judgment about which sources are sufficiently credible, which claims are disputed, which evidence belongs in the script, and which information should be excluded remains under human editorial review.

RESEARCH RESPONSIBILITY

The credibility of a good documentary begins before the story itself

Strong narration, cinematic visuals, and engaging editing can make a documentary compelling. But if its factual foundation is weak, the production cannot remain credible in the long term.

SalimGPT therefore treats source discovery, source quality, cross-checking, chronology, statistical context, uncertainty, and final human review as one connected research process.
