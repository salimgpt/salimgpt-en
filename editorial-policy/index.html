EDITORIAL POLICY

Make the story fit the evidence— not the evidence fit the story

SalimGPT's documentary editorial policy is built on evidence, context, source quality, and human judgment. Information is not selected to prove a predetermined conclusion. Instead, the documentary narrative should follow where the research leads.

CORE PRINCIPLE

Let the information go where the evidence leads

SalimGPT does not begin a documentary with a predetermined conclusion that the research must ultimately prove. If new evidence, conflicting sources, or new historical context emerge during research, the narrative direction may change.

Documentary storytelling requires structure, pacing, and interpretation. But excluding evidence, hiding contradictory sources, or creating unsupported certainty for the convenience of storytelling is not editorially acceptable.

SalimGPT's editorial goal: compelling story + defensible evidence + clear context.

TOPIC SELECTION

Not only viral potential— editorial value also matters

SalimGPT may cover history, science, society, education, technology, mystery, investigation, human civilization, and other documentary-friendly subjects.

When selecting a topic, the decision is not based only on curiosity or trends. Research value, public interest, historical significance, explanatory potential, and the availability of reliable sources are also considered.

Creating a sensational headline may be easy, but the primary value of a documentary should be helping audiences understand the subject more clearly.

EVIDENCE-LED EDITING

The narrative should follow the evidence

Beginning with a conclusion before research and selecting only sources that support that conclusion can create confirmation bias.

SalimGPT's policy is to consider contradictory evidence, alternative explanations, and uncertainty alongside supporting evidence.

The final documentary structure may be clear and engaging, but factual complexity should not be simplified in a misleading way.

EDITORIAL NEUTRALITY

Neutrality does not mean treating every side as equally correct

SalimGPT's editorial neutrality is not based on the idea that because two sides exist, both must be “50-50 true.”

If the evidence supporting one position is strong while another claim is weak or disproven, there is no need to give both positions equal evidentiary weight simply to create symmetry.

Editorial fairness means not intentionally excluding relevant evidence, context, credible disagreement, or important counter-evidence.

PRINCIPLE Evidence determines weight

Editorial emphasis should be determined by the strength of the evidence, not by the number of sides.

CONTENT DISTINCTION

Fact, analysis, and interpretation are not the same thing

FACT

Verifiable Information

Dates, events, records, measurements, documented statements, or evidence-supported claims.

ANALYSIS

Interpretation of Evidence

Explaining relationships, causes, or broader meaning among different facts.

INTERPRETATION

Contextual Understanding

Explanations based on historical, scholarly, or editorial interpretation.

CONTEXT MATTERS

Even accurate information can be misleading without context

A statistic, quotation, historical event, or scientific finding can create a misleading impression if separated from its original context.

SalimGPT's editorial review may therefore include the relevant year, location, historical background, methodology, scope, and limitations when necessary.

UNCERTAINTY

Uncertain information should not be presented as certain

ESTIMATED

If a number is an estimate, describe it as an estimate.

REPORTED

If a claim comes from a source, retain the source context.

DISPUTED

If credible disagreement exists, acknowledge the dispute.

UNKNOWN

If evidence is unavailable, saying “unknown” is acceptable.

EDITORIAL REVIEW

Not only the script— the entire documentary is reviewed

The final meaning of a documentary is not created by narration alone. Visuals, music, timing, editing pace, title, and thumbnail can all influence audience interpretation.

Even if the factual script is accurate, misleading visuals, an exaggerated thumbnail, a deceptive title, or context-changing edits can create editorial problems.

SalimGPT's final review considers the documentary's combined presentation.

TITLE & THUMBNAIL

Attention is acceptable— deception is not

Strong Title

A title may create curiosity, but it should not make a fundamentally false promise about the documentary's content.

Compelling Thumbnail

A thumbnail may be dramatic, but it should not mislead viewers with fabricated factual claims or unrelated scenes.

Content Match

The title, thumbnail, and documentary narrative should have a substantive connection.

CARE REQUIRED
SENSITIVE SUBJECTS

For sensitive subjects, context matters more than curiosity

When covering war, violence, drugs, crime, exploitation, sexuality, death, public-health issues, or human suffering, sensational framing may increase real-world harm.

Factual detail, terminology, victim dignity, visual intensity, and unnecessary graphic material require particular consideration.

The purpose of a documentary is not to create shock value; it is to explain the subject.

SCIENCE & EVIDENCE

The level of certainty matters in scientific claims

A single paper, preliminary finding, or observational result should not automatically be presented as scientific consensus.

Research quality, study design, sample, replication, review status, and the broader scientific evidence may be considered whenever possible.

AI SUPPORT
AI & EDITORIAL CONTROL

AI may provide editorial assistance, but it cannot take editorial responsibility

AI may assist with outlines, wording, translation, research organization, visual generation, or review.

But decisions about which information is accurate, which sources are credible, which wording is fair, which visuals may be misleading, and whether the final documentary should be published remain under human editorial control.

VISUAL EDITORIAL STANDARD

Visuals are not a substitute for truth

A documentary may use AI-generated scenes, archival images, licensed footage, maps, graphics, or conceptual visuals.

But a visual should not create a factual impression that is unsupported by the narration or evidence.

Historical reconstructions, AI-generated scenes, or illustrative sequences should not be misleadingly used as authentic historical camera footage.

VISUAL RULE Illustrate evidence, do not manufacture evidence.
FIX WHEN NEEDED
CORRECTIONS

Correcting errors is part of editorial responsibility

SalimGPT does not present itself as infallible or as a platform that can never make mistakes.

If credible evidence identifies a factual error after publication, the article, description, future edition, or related content may be corrected when necessary.

When new evidence changes an earlier interpretation, considering updated information is part of editorial honesty.

ACCURACY WITHOUT FALSE CERTAINTY

Avoid universal claims such as “100% verified”

Every historical, scientific, or statistical detail in a documentary may not always be known with absolute certainty.

Sources may be incomplete, academic interpretations may change, and new evidence may revise earlier understanding.

SalimGPT therefore aims to produce documentaries that are as accurate, transparent, and defensible as possible according to the evidence available at the time of publication.

CONFLICT OF INTEREST

Relevant relationships and their influence on editorial judgment should be considered

If a documentary topic, source, partner, or third-party relationship could meaningfully influence editorial independence, it becomes a matter for internal review.

Editorial conclusions should not be changed because of payment, sponsorship, access, or external pressure.
